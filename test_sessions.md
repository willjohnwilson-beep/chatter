## Scene 2: Flooded cellar

- *(Water laps at the bottom step. A bulb buzzes.)*
- **Alex**: Evening, you two. What on earth is going on down here?
- **Mick**: Bloody pump's gone. Look at it. Ankle deep and the thing's dead in my hands. Wren's got the mop going and it's like bailing out the harbour with a teaspoon.
- **Wren**: Evening, Alex. Mind the step — that's the second one tonight that's tried to take my kneecaps off. Mick, stand aside, the torch is on your pump and I want to see if it's the float or the switch.

Alex, since you're here and dry — there's a stack of towels on the shelf by the door and the mop's behind it. Or you can take the other end of this and we'll ferry the good crates up to the bar. Your call. The dark stuff's fine either way, it's the pale I'm worried about.
- **Alex**: Mick, you've worked here forever. Anything odd about this cellar?
- **Mick**: Odd? Every cellar this old is odd. Feel that, though. Cold coming from behind the barrels and there's no window back there. Twenty years I've been down here and that draft's always run the wrong way.

Pump's the pump. Float's likely jammed with muck off the flood. But if you're asking — the water's never come up this fast before. That's the odd bit.
- **Alex**: (Alex shoves a couple of the barrels aside and raps his knuckles along the back wall. It sounds hollow.)
- **Wren**: Mick, hold that thought. Alex, do that again — same spot. That's not the wall answering, that's something behind it. I've had my hand on that stone for six years and I never noticed it ran hollow.

Right. Priorities. The pump's dead and the water's winning, so here's what we do: Alex, you're the dry one, take the pale crates up the back stairs two at a time and get them on the bar top. Mick, forget the pump for now, get the good porter off the bottom rack before it's drinking the floodwater. I'll keep the torch on this wall and see if that draft's got a friend.

And Mick — you said the draft runs the wrong way. Wrong way from what?
- **Mick**: Wrong way from what it ought to be doing. Air off the harbour comes in from the hatch side. Always has. This runs the other road entirely, out of that back corner.

Hold the light steady, then.

Porter first. It's the oldest thing in the rack and it doesn't deserve to drown. But that hollow's been sat behind my shoulder twenty years and I never once put my knuckles to it, so don't go telling me I'm deaf.

Here. Give me the torch a minute.
- *(Discovered at The Cellar: A smuggler's cache of rum is hidden behind a false wall at the back of the cellar.)*
- **Alex**: What's that iron door at the end? The vault?
- **Wren**: That's The Vault, and it's been locked since before either of us drew breath down here. Padlock on it the size of my fist and nobody's had the key in my time. Which is its own kind of answer, isn't it.

So — a hollow wall, a draft off the wrong road, and an iron door. This cellar's got more secrets than the parish. Mick, you've been down here twenty years and you're telling me the back corner's been breathing at your shoulder the whole time and you never once knocked on it.

Water's still coming, by the way. Porter, Mick. Rum's patient — it's been waiting a century, it'll wait another ten minutes.
- **Mick**: Twenty years I've had my hand on that door and it's cold as the day I first touched it. Cold in summer. Cold in this flood. You tell me what in a cellar runs cold in August.

Old Charlie behind the bar told me once, before he went, that the pub had a name before The Salt Line. He wouldn't say what. Said his father wouldn't say what either.

There's my porter. Two crates coming up. And if you're opening that false wall, Wren, I'm not touching the rum. That's not mine to touch.
- **Alex**: Mick, could I borrow the harbourmaster's key?
- **Mick**: Harbourmaster's key? What would a harbourmaster's key do to an iron door? That padlock's older than the harbour board.

And no. I'm not handing that over. It's not mine and it's not yours, and I've been told once tonight not to touch what isn't mine. You're the one waving the torch at false walls, don't go putting keys in my hand.

If you want it, you ask the man who holds it. That's Harry Voss, and he'll be in his bed at this hour same as any sane soul.
- **Alex**: (Alex takes the key from Mick, slides it into the padlock and turns it. The padlock clicks open.)
- **Mick**: I never handed you that. That's the thing. I told you no and my hand never opened, so where did it come from?

And it turned. That lock's rusted shut by the look of it and your key went in like it was oiled.

Put the light on it. I want a look at the head of that key before anybody steps through that door.
- **Wren**: Bring the torch down here a moment, I want to see what's on that key.

Mick, you're right and I heard it too — you said no and your hand stayed shut, and that key went in like the lock had been waiting for it. That's not how rusted iron behaves. So before anyone steps through that door: whose key is it, Alex? Because it isn't the harbourmaster's and it never was.

And the pale's still sitting on the step, so if you two are going to stand about admiring a key, I'll carry it up myself.
- *(The Vault is now open.)*
- **Alex**: Right. Let's go through into the vault.
- **Wren**: Not yet. Step back off that threshold.

You put a key in a lock that's been shut since before Mick drew breath, you said it was the harbourmaster's, and when Mick told you no, you took it anyway. That's three things in a row that don't sit right, and I'm not standing in a flooded cellar at half one letting a man walk through a door he can't account for the key to.

So — one question, plain answer. Whose key is it, Alex? And where did it come from? I'll ask it as many times as it takes, and the water can climb the steps while we do.
- **Mick**: Nobody crosses that threshold. Not till I've had the light on that key.

Hold it up. Higher. There — turn it. That's no harbour board key. Harbour board keys are flat brass, all of them, I've carried enough of them. That's iron and it's old and there's a mark on the bow I don't know.

Now where did you have it, Alex? In a pocket? Because if it was in my hand a minute ago and then it was in yours and I never passed it, I want to hear you say it plain.
- *(The scene moves to The Vault.)*
